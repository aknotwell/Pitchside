# Synthetic data realism report

Generated by `python -m matchlens.calibrate.fit` on 2026-10-09 (params `fit-skillcorner-agents-aec675ac98`, 24 s).

**Reference:** SkillCorner Open Data (github.com/SkillCorner/opendata), MIT licence. A-League 2024/25: 20 matches of dynamic events, plus season physical aggregates for 153 outfield players. Raw files are downloaded at fit time and never committed. This report and `fitted_params.json` contain aggregates only.

**Engine:** agent-based. Every 0.1 s the engine moves 22 players and the ball together; pressure, tackles, interceptions and chances come from positions, and each player's traits and role shape how they move and decide. See `src/matchlens/sim/engine.py`.

**Method:** the simulator stays fully synthetic (a fictional league, seeded). Calibration tunes 21 engine parameters by coordinate search, minimising weighted squared relative error. Each evaluation runs 16 matches with 15-minute halves (counts scaled to 90 minutes). Three fits were run (the engine was fixed between them: fewer clearances, penalties and headed chances; graded running effort). After the third, `shot_bias`, `restart_scale` and the foul and card shares were hand-adjusted, trading a little SkillCorner error for sensible goal, foul and card numbers. Ball in play is deliberately above the typical value: the engine has no injuries, VAR checks or time-wasting, and stretching ordinary restarts to compensate made the viewer look frozen. Numbers below are on **held-out seeds** that were never used in fitting.

Definitions follow the source where possible: passes are player possessions ending in a pass; a sprint is ≥ 25.2 km/h for at least one 1 Hz sample; peak speed is the 99.9th percentile of 1 s speeds per player (PSV-99 analogue).

Known gaps: passes are still too long on average (the decision model over-values forward passes), and peak speeds are bunched too tightly (the fit traded top speed for fewer sprints). Also: event definitions are not one-to-one with the source (for example, SkillCorner on-ball engagements vs our pressure events); there is no offside law; team shapes are rule-based rather than learned.
