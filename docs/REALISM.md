# Synthetic data realism report

Generated by `python -m matchlens.calibrate.fit` on 2026-10-10 (params `fit-skillcorner-agents-f3bd95b62c`, 179 s).

**Reference:** SkillCorner Open Data (github.com/SkillCorner/opendata), MIT licence. A-League 2024/25: 20 matches of dynamic events, plus season physical aggregates for 153 outfield players. Raw files are downloaded at fit time and never committed. This report and `fitted_params.json` contain aggregates only.

**Engine:** agent-based. Every 0.1 s the engine moves 22 players and the ball together; pressure, tackles, interceptions and chances come from positions, and each player's traits and role shape how they move and decide. See `src/matchlens/sim/engine.py`.

**Method:** the simulator stays fully synthetic (a fictional league, seeded). Calibration tunes 31 engine parameters by coordinate search, minimising weighted squared relative error. Each evaluation runs 64 matches with 15-minute halves (counts scaled to 90 minutes). After the automatic fit, decision time, carrying, tackling, crossing, the shot model, top speeds and home advantage were hand-tuned and checked on 64–256 unseen matches each time, because goals and shots are too noisy for the automatic search. Numbers below are on **64 held-out matches** that were never used in fitting. Those seeds and all 20 real matches were also used while hand-tuning, so this first table is optimistic. The **test set** section below is the honest check: real matches and players held out by a fixed seed, and simulated seeds that tuning never saw. From now on, hand-tuning should use the validation split and seeds 501+ (see `calibrate/fit.py`).

Definitions follow the source where possible: passes are player possessions ending in a pass (open play; restart deliveries such as throw-ins and goal kicks are not player possessions in SkillCorner either); a sprint is ≥ 25.2 km/h for at least one 1 Hz sample; peak speed is the 99.9th percentile of 1 s speeds per player (PSV-99 analogue).

Known gaps: peak speed and pass length are a few percent low, and passes per match a few percent high. Also: event definitions are not one-to-one with the source (for example, SkillCorner on-ball engagements vs our pressure events); there is no offside law; team shapes are rule-based rather than learned.
